  • Annotation Transfer: SAE features as a Rosetta Stone for metagenomic dark matter

The question

Can we use what each SAE feature has already learnt about SwissProt — its InterPro fold assignment, its MEME sequence motif, the backbone-geometry rule that triggers it — to annotate NMPFam families that currently have no or weak Pfam hits? If a feature fires on both a well-annotated SwissProt protein and on a novel metagenomic family, the annotation of the former is a defensible hypothesis for the latter.

Setup: NMPFamsDB [Baltoumas 2024] provides ~50k families with AlphaFold2 structures [Jumper 2021]. Consensus sequences are embedded with ESM‑2 [Lin 2023] and passed through a layer‑4 sparse autoencoder trained in the InterPLM style [Simon & Zou 2024]. Per‑feature evidence is assembled from InterPro [Paysan-Lafosse 2023], MEME‑PWM sweeps [Bailey 2015], and a gradient-boosted classifier trained on per-residue backbone descriptors (curvature, torsion, tangent alignment, end-to-end ratio, contact density). Significance is Benjamini–Hochberg at q < 0.05 [Benjamini & Hochberg 1995].

Metric glossary. protF1 = best F1 at protein-level InterPro prediction. resF1 = best F1 predicting the annotated residues within a protein. norm act = NMPFam max activation / feature global max (values >1 mean the metagenomic family exceeds every SwissProt protein). resAUROC = geometry-classifier per-residue AUROC. The geometric feature names come from the backbone descriptor set; a one-line interpretation accompanies each rule.

Confidence tiering (q-value-driven)

Every NMPFam hit is assigned a tier from the BH-corrected per-method q-values of its parent SAE feature combined with NMPFam normalised activation. No F1 / AUC thresholds are used — the tiers are computed from the same permutation-null q‑values that drive the rest of the paper's significance reporting.

A ≥6/7 methods q<0.05 · norm act ≥0.70
B ≥4/7 methods q<0.05 · norm act ≥0.55
C ≥2/7 methods q<0.05 · norm act ≥0.50
D otherwise (above SAE act threshold)

The seven methods are InterPro protein, InterPro residue, CATH protein, CATH residue, sequence position, MEME motif, and geometry — all scored with BH [Benjamini & Hochberg 1995]. A-tier hits are recommended annotations; D-tier are speculative hypotheses only.

A. Fold-level annotation transfer

Features whose SwissProt activation matches a specific InterPro family (protein-level F1 ≥ 0.85) and which also fire on previously uncharacterised metagenomic families. The fold label transfers as a working hypothesis. Geometry and MEME evidence corroborate the call.

B. Functional-site (residue-level) transfer

Residue-level InterPro concordance is much harder than protein-level: it says that the feature fires precisely on the catalytic/binding residues, not just inside the domain. Where that signal is strong (resF1 ≥ 0.60), the NMPFam hit can be annotated at the site level — proposed catalytic or ligand-binding residues, not just a fold class.

C. Repeat-scaffold annotation at scale

Solenoid/propeller repeat proteins (TPR [D'Andrea & Regan 2003], WD40 [Stirnimann 2010], ANK [Mosavi 2004], LRR [Kobe & Kajava 2001]) are a sizeable slice of the metagenomic repeat-protein dark matter. A single SAE feature per scaffold can annotate tens to hundreds of NMPFams.

D. Sequence-motif-anchored transfer (MEME)

Features with a crisp MEME consensus [Bailey 2015] that scores well on NMPFams. Very small E-values or high PR-AUCs indicate that the feature is decoupled from the fold identity and is primarily picking up a sequence signal that travels.

E. Pure-geometry features — novel structural-motif classes

Features where geometry is significant (q < 0.02) but the InterPro match is weak (protF1 < 0.50). The feature is picking up a shape rather than a known family name, so the NMPFam hits become candidate members of a structural motif class rather than an existing domain. Biologically these are the most exciting — they identify uncharted fold-space rather than re-labelling it.

Caveats

  • NMPFam consensus sequences are derived from multiple metagenomic homologues [Baltoumas 2024]; the AlphaFold2 model is of the consensus, not of an experimentally solved structure. Treat high norm act as a hypothesis, not a fact.
  • SAE features are polysemantic in the worst case. A high per-residue AUROC plus a specific geometric rule raises confidence that the feature is doing one thing rather than many [Cunningham 2023].
  • Residue-level F1 cannot be computed for the ~50k NMPFams directly (they lack InterPro residue annotations); we transfer the SwissProt-measured site-specificity and verify by inspecting per-residue activation on the top NMPFam consensus.
  • Protein-level F1 on SwissProt can be inflated by family size. For the fold-transfer call we require protF1 ≥ 0.85 and a coherent geometric rule, not protF1 alone.
